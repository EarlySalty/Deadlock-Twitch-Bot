Server setup guide

Add the Deadlock Twitch bot to your community

This takes about ten minutes. You invite the bot, run one command and your streamers sign up through a short login page. After that, live posts and raids run on their own.

Testing phase. Multi-server support is built but not battle-tested yet. Expect rough edges and tell us what breaks. During the test, invite links are handed out personally: message Nani on Discord and you get the link for your server.

What your server gets

Go-live posts

A post in your Discord channel when one of your streamers goes live, only when they play Deadlock.

Auto-raids

When a streamer goes offline, the bot raids another live streamer of your community.

Chat commands

Commands like !rank in the Twitch chat. Full list.

Streamer dashboard

Stats and settings for each streamer. A social media manager for clips is in beta.

What you need

  • A Discord server where you have Manage Server or Administrator.
  • A text channel for the live posts.
  • A few Deadlock streamers who want to take part.
  • One contact person, for example a mod, who can make decisions for the server.
  • Optional: a streamer role and a role people can ping for live posts.

Setup in five steps

  1. 1

    Invite the bot

    Open the invite link you got from Nani and pick your server. The bot only asks for these permissions:

    • View Channels
    • Send Messages
    • Embed Links
    • Manage Roles, only to give streamers your streamer role

    It does not ask for Administrator and cannot ping @everyone.

  2. 2

    Fix the role order

    Go to Server Settings → Roles and drag the bot's role above your streamer role and your ping role. Discord only lets a bot manage roles that sit below its own.

    The ping role must have "Allow anyone to @mention this role" turned on.

  3. 3

    Run /setup

    Type /setup in any channel. Only members with Manage Server can use it. Fill in the options you want; all of them are optional and you can run /setup again anytime to change something.

    OptionWhat it does
    languageGerman or English for the bot's messages on your server.
    announce_channelChannel for the go-live posts.
    alert_channelChannel for moderation alerts, ideally only visible to your mods.
    streamer_roleRole the bot gives to your partnered streamers.
    ping_roleRole that gets pinged in live posts.
    inviteInvite link to your Discord, used when the bot promotes your server.
    auto_partnerOn (default): streamers who sign up are active right away. Off: new streamers stay pending until Nani activates them for you.

    The bot answers with a permission check that only you can see. If something is missing, it tells you what, and nothing is saved until the check passes.

  4. 4

    Let your streamers sign up

    Send your streamers this link: deutsche-deadlock-community.de/twitch/guild-onboarding

    • Log in with Twitch.
    • Log in with Discord. This proves they are a member of your server.
    • Pick your server from the list.

    With auto_partner on they are active immediately. Otherwise they stay pending; tell Nani who should be activated.

  5. 5

    Do a test stream

    Have one streamer go live in Deadlock. The post should show up in your announce channel within a few minutes. If not, send Nani your server name and the time of the stream.

If something goes wrong

"You need Manage Server permission."
Only members with Manage Server or Administrator can run /setup.
"The channel is not in this server or the bot cannot view it…"
Give the bot View Channel, Send Messages and Embed Links in that channel, then run /setup again.
"The streamer role was not saved."
The bot's role sits below your streamer role. Move it up (step 2) and run /setup again.
"The ping role was not saved."
The role is not mentionable or sits above the bot's role. @everyone cannot be used as ping role.
A streamer does not see your server in the list
They have to be a member of your Discord with the same Discord account they log in with.

Good to know

  • AI features stay off on partner servers. This is enforced by a database rule, not just a setting.
  • If a streamer leaves your Discord, their membership for your server ends automatically.
  • If you remove the bot, all memberships for your server end. Adding it back later does not restore them; streamers sign up again.
  • Streamers can be part of several communities at the same time.
